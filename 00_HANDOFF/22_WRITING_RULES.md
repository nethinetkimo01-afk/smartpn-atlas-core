## 1. Reader Simulation Rule

Never assume the reader reads from first word to last word.
The reader is a busy CEO or industry professional.
Their eyes SCAN first. They stop at words that interest them. Then they deep-read.

Write for the scanner first, the deep reader second.

---

## 2. Keyword Placement Rule

Every key concept must be placed at a position where scanning eyes will stop:
- End of a short paragraph
- Beginning of a new paragraph
- On its own line

Key concepts for SmartPN Atlas:
- STANDARD ZERO ZONE
- SHARED LANGUAGE
- GOVERNANCE LAYER
- TRUSTED UPSTREAM SOURCE
- INNOVATIVE CONCEPT AND SOLUTION

---

## 3. Capitalization Rule

Use ALL CAPS for words or phrases that must stop the reader's eye.
This replaces color or bold formatting in plain text (email, LinkedIn).

Examples:
- SHARED LANGUAGE (not Shared Language)
- GOVERNANCE LAYER (not governance layer)
- STANDARD ZERO ZONE (not Standard Zero Zone)
- NO ONE WOULD LIKE TO SHARE THEIR BUSINESS SECRETS
- TRUSTED UPSTREAM SOURCE
- INNOVATIVE CONCEPT AND SOLUTION
- SHARED LANGUAGE + GOVERNANCE LAYER

Rule: If a concept is a hook or a key takeaway, it gets ALL CAPS.

---

## 4. Hook Rule

Hooks are placed to create curiosity, not to explain.
A hook makes the reader want to know more.

Example of correct hook usage:
"And I want to share an INNOVATIVE CONCEPT AND SOLUTION ??I hope after reading this, you'll feel the same way."

NOT: "I want to share my innovative concept and solution, which I believe is innovative."
The first version makes the reader curious. The second tells them what to think.

---

## 5. Logic Chain Rule

Every piece of writing must follow Jim's logic chain structure:
Problem ??Obvious solution ??Why it's not enough ??Hidden risk ??Real innovation ??SmartPN Atlas

Never skip steps. Never reorder steps.
Each step must end with a hook that pulls the reader into the next step.

Example:
- No SHARED LANGUAGE (problem)
- Give every material a barcode (obvious solution)
- Only solves half the problem (not enough)
- Barcode = 100% open data = NO ONE SHARES BUSINESS SECRETS (hidden risk)
- SHARED LANGUAGE + GOVERNANCE LAYER (real innovation)
- SmartPN Atlas (the answer)

---

## 6. Sentence Length Rule

Short sentences create impact.
After a long explanation, use one short sentence to land the point.

Example:
"That works for consumers. But in the manufacturing side?
Definitely not."

"Definitely not." on its own line = maximum impact.

---

## 7. Benefits Must Be Concrete Rule

When describing what SmartPN Atlas does for the reader,
make them visualize their own situation.

Wrong: "SmartPN Atlas improves data quality."
Right: "Right now, you don't know if factories are using 100% correct materials.
With SHARED LANGUAGE established, a factory cannot use the wrong material ??unless intentionally."

The reader must see themselves in the scenario.

---

## 8. CEO Perspective Rule

Always write from the CEO's perspective, not from the product's perspective.

Wrong: "SmartPN Atlas provides a governance layer that manages material identity."
Right: "If you are a CEO of a brand group ??with multiple brands and multiple product lines ??imagine how much this changes for you."

The CEO must feel: "This person understands my world."
